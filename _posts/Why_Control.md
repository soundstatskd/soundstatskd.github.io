When I first started consulting at the University of Washington in 2013, my faculty advisor told me that my most critical contribution would involve explaining why studies should be controlled.  While I supported many other aspects of study design and analysis, I found this insight about the tendency for non-academics to conduct uncontrolled (pre-post) studies to hold true.
Though awareness of causal inference has increased beyond biostatistics, many medical researchers remain naive to benefits of study designs that include external control conditions or groups.  In this post, I provide some examples from the literature that reflect my recent experience with clients.
